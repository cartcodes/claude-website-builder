# Conversion: the reason every page exists

Every landing page has one job: get the visitor to take its one action. SEO, AI search, social posts and ads only bring people to the page. The page succeeds when they book, submit, download, register or buy. When choosing between something that helps ranking and something that helps conversion, choose conversion, then find a way to keep both.

Resource pages, reached from content or social, are judged almost entirely on this. Search pages have to rank *and* convert.

## What makes a page convert (check every page against this list)

**Match**
- The headline repeats what the visitor came for: their search, the ad, or the social post that sent them. The same words and the same promise. A mismatch is the biggest single reason people leave.
- One page, one offer, one action. Every extra choice lowers the chance they take the one that matters.

**Clarity in five seconds**
- A stranger looking at the first screen for five seconds can say what it is, who it's for, and what to do. Test this out loud before publishing.
- The button says what they get ("Get my free quote", "Send me the checklist", "Save my seat"), not "Submit" or "Learn more".

**Low friction**
- Ask for as little as possible. Each extra form field costs conversions. Name and email (or phone) is often enough. Ask the rest on the call or after the conversion. Four visible fields is the ceiling unless qualification really requires more. The checker warns above that.
- On a phone, the action is reachable without zooming, the form uses the right keyboard (email, phone), and nothing covers it (no pop-ups, chat bubbles over the button, or cookie walls that hide it).
- The page loads fast (see `technical-seo.md`). Slow pages lose visitors before they read a word.
- Booking widgets and forms open instantly. If an embed is slow, a fast button opens it.

**Proof next to the ask**
- Put the strongest trust right beside or under the button: rating and review count, a short testimonial with a name and a specific result, a guarantee, a recognisable client or credential.
- Proof that matches the visitor: a testimonial from their place, industry or situation beats a generic one.

**Objections answered before they're asked**
- Price: show the range, or how pricing works.
- Risk: state the guarantee, cancellation or refund terms plainly. "No obligation", "Free, takes 2 minutes", "We'll never share your email" near the form.
- Time and effort: what happens next and how long it takes.
- Trust: who is behind this (a real person with a photo works).
- The FAQs below the fold should be the real questions people ask before buying.

**Honest urgency only**
- Real deadlines (event date, limited seats, seasonal timing) are good to show. Fake countdown timers and invented scarcity are not allowed. They damage trust and break consumer protection rules.

**The success page keeps it going**
- It delivers instantly what was promised and offers one natural next step. A guide download offers the call. A booked call offers the short video that prepares them.

## Measure it

- Conversion rate per landing page = success page views ÷ landing page views (over the same period). Cloudflare Web Analytics, or the analytics tool the owner already uses, shows both numbers because every goal ends on its own success page.
- Track by traffic source too (search, each social platform, ads, email), since the same page can convert very differently by source.
- Log results monthly in `research/conversion.md`: page, visitors, conversions, rate, source, and any change made.
- Rough guide for a new page with enough traffic to judge (around 100+ visitors): gated lead magnets often convert 20-40% of visitors from warm social traffic, quote and booking pages 3-10% from search. Use these only to spot pages that are far off. The owner's own numbers are the real benchmark.

## Improve it (one change at a time)

When a page has traffic but converts poorly, change one thing, then wait for enough visitors to compare. In this order, because this is where the biggest gains usually are:
1. **The headline**: does it match what visitors came for, in their words?
2. **The offer**: is the thing being offered worth the action? A better lead magnet or a clearer free consultation beats any wording change.
3. **The form and button**: fewer fields, a clearer button, less friction on mobile.
4. **The proof**: stronger, more specific, more relevant trust next to the button.
5. **Objections**: answer the reason people hesitate (ask the owner what prospects say on calls).

Write each change and its result in `research/conversion.md`, so the next change builds on what was learned. Because every page comes from a contract and a content file, a change that works on one page can be rolled out to every page in its archive.

# Copywriting: plain, specific, built to convert

The visitor has a problem and a few seconds. Good copy shows them, in their own words, that this business solves exactly that problem, proves it with facts, and tells them what to do next. It reads like a capable person talking across a table, not like a brochure. Its job is the conversion (`conversion.md`).

## The principles

1. **Research before writing.** Great copy comes from facts about the business, not from clever wording. Before writing a page, list the facts you have for it: prices, timelines, what's included, guarantees, credentials, numbers served, specific results, details about the place or audience, real customer quotes. If the list is thin, ask the owner for more before writing.
2. **The headline does most of the work.** Most visitors read only the headline. It names the reader's problem or the outcome they want, in their words, matches what sent them here, includes the main keyword on search pages, and makes a specific promise. "[Outcome] in [timeframe], without [common pain]" beats "Quality you can trust".
3. **Facts sell. Adjectives don't.** "Same-week appointments, 4.9 stars from 312 reviews" beats "trusted and convenient". "Most projects cost $X to $Y" beats "affordable pricing". "Set up in 20 minutes" beats "easy to use". Every claim gets a number, a name or a detail. If it can't be made specific, cut it.
4. **Talk to one person.** "You" and "your [thing]", never "customers", "users" or "clients in the area". Use the words customers use when they call, email or comment, not industry jargon.
5. **Problem first, then solution.** Open with what is going wrong for them, in their words. Then show how the business fixes it, step by step. Name the cost of waiting honestly, without scare tactics.
6. **Proof beats promises.** Testimonials with name, place or role, and a specific result. Photos of real work and real people. Numbers only this business has. A guarantee stated plainly.
7. **Tell them exactly what to do and what happens next.** "Book a free 15-minute call. You'll get a calendar invite right away, and we'll send three questions so the call is useful." The button says what they get.
8. **Long enough to answer every real question, never longer.** Below the first screen, cover what a careful buyer wants to know (cost, time, process, risk, why this business), then stop. Every sentence must inform or persuade. If it does neither, delete it. Resource pages can be short: show what they get, prove it's worth it, ask.
9. **Subheads that tell the story on their own.** Someone who only skims the subheads should still understand the offer: "What it costs", "How long it takes", "What's included", "What happens if you're not happy".
10. **Never boring, never cute.** No puns, no wordplay that hides the meaning, no hype. Clear wins.

## Page blueprint

**First screen** (see `site-structure.md`):
- Headline: problem or outcome, in their words, plus the main keyword on search pages, plus a specific promise.
- Sub text, two lines max: who it's for and the strongest proof.
- The call to action, with the outcome in the button, plus one line on what happens next.
- Trust next to the action: badges and 2-3 one-sentence testimonials with specific results.
- Resource pages: the preview of what they get.

**Below the first screen**, in the order the page's contract lists, skipping anything that adds nothing. The usual shape:
1. The problem in the reader's words (2-4 short sentences).
2. How it gets solved: 3-5 numbered steps, each with a concrete detail.
3. Proof specific to this page (its place, audience or offering).
4. Price and time: ranges, what changes the price, how long it takes.
5. Why this business, as facts.
6. Objections answered (risk, effort, trust).
7. FAQs: real questions with answer-first replies (`geo-aeo.md`, section 3).
8. The same call to action again.
9. A small related-pages block.

**Place pages and offering-in-place pages** must earn their place with genuinely local specifics: work done there, local rules or conditions, what customers there ask, a testimonial from there, local price notes. If the only difference from another page would be the place name, don't build the page.

**Audience pages** use that audience's words, problems and results. A testimonial from that audience is worth more than any paragraph.

**Resource pages** (lead magnets, events): the headline names what they get and the problem it solves. Show it (preview), list what's inside with specifics and numbers, say who it's for, prove it's worth having, and ask for as little as possible.

## Keywords in copy (search pages)

- Main keyword in: title, H1, URL, first 100 words, one subhead, one image alt text and file name, meta description.
- Secondary keywords in subheads and FAQ questions, where they read naturally.
- Write for the person first. If a keyword makes a sentence clumsy, rephrase the sentence, not the keyword. Never repeat a keyword just to repeat it.
- Don't write "near me" into copy. Search engines handle that from the visitor's location. Name the real places instead.

## Removing AI tells (run on every page before showing it)

AI-written copy is easy to spot, and readers and search engines both discount it. Fix all of these.

**The core fix: replace every vague claim with a specific fact, and every abstract description with a concrete example.** Ask: could a stranger tell this was written by someone who knows this business? If not, rewrite it.

**Words to replace** (use the plain word, or cut the sentence): delve, tapestry, realm, landscape (as a metaphor), embark, journey (as a metaphor), multifaceted, robust, leverage, utilize, streamline, foster, enhance, elevate, empower, unlock, harness, navigate (as a metaphor), pivotal, comprehensive, seamless, cutting-edge, innovative, groundbreaking, unprecedented, transformative, game-changer, holistic, paradigm, synergy, bespoke, meticulous, intricate, paramount, testament, myriad, plethora, vibrant, bustling, nestled, top-notch, world-class, second to none, one-stop shop.

**Adverbs to delete**: seamlessly, meticulously, significantly, notably, crucially, importantly, additionally, consequently, effortlessly, truly, really, very.

**Phrases to delete**:
- Throat-clearing: "In today's fast-paced world", "When it comes to", "It's important to note", "Let's dive in", "Whether you're a X, a Y, or a Z".
- Stock business lines: "We pride ourselves on", "Your one-stop shop", "Peace of mind", "Look no further", "We go above and beyond", "Quality you can trust", "Customer satisfaction is our top priority".
- Formal transitions: "Furthermore", "Moreover", "Additionally" at the start of a sentence. Use "and", "also", "so" or "but".
- Conclusions: "In conclusion", "In summary", "Ultimately". End on the last real point or the call to action.

**Patterns to break**:
- "It's not X, it's Y" and "Not just X, but Y". Say what it is.
- Lists of exactly three adjectives or benefits every time. Use the real number: two, four, five.
- Every sentence the same length. Mix short sentences with longer ones, but keep the average under about 16 words.
- Paragraphs that all go topic sentence, explanation, example, summary.
- Objects doing the action ("Our process ensures..."). Use people: "We check every [detail] before we finish."
- "Serves as", "boasts", "features" instead of "is" and "has".
- Cycling synonyms for the same thing (experts, professionals, specialists, team). Pick one word and repeat it.

**Formatting**:
- No em dashes. Use a full stop, comma or colon.
- Bold only where it changes the meaning. No emoji bullets. No decorative arrows.
- Lists only for things that really are lists (steps, what's included).

**Readability target**: most sentences under 20 words, paragraphs of 1-3 sentences, everyday words a 12-year-old knows, active voice, contractions allowed (you'll, we're, don't).

If the owner has a `voice.md`, follow it on top of these rules.

The site checker flags em dashes, the words above, stock phrases and long average sentences. A clean checker run is the minimum, not proof that the copy is good. Read every page out loud. If it sounds like an ad or a robot, rewrite it.
